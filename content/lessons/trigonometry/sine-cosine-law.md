## When the triangle is not right-angled
Label the angles $A$, $B$, $C$ and the sides opposite them $a$, $b$, $c$.

## Sine law
Use it when you know an angle and the side opposite it, plus one more side or angle.

$$\frac{a}{\sin A} = \frac{b}{\sin B} = \frac{c}{\sin C}$$

## Cosine law
Use it when you know two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides.

$$c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos C$$

To find an angle from three sides, rearrange it:

$$\cos C = \frac{a^2 + b^2 - c^2}{2ab}$$

The largest angle is opposite the longest side.

## Area from two sides and the included angle
$$\text{Area} = \tfrac{1}{2}ab\sin C$$

## Worked example
Two sides of a triangle are $a = 12$ m and $b = 9$ m, and the angle between them is $C = 50°$. Find the third side.

- Two sides and the included angle: cosine law.
- $c^2 = 12^2 + 9^2 - 2(12)(9)\cos 50° = 225 - 216(0.6428) = 86.16$
- $c \approx 9.28$ m.

## Common slips
- Using the sine law to find an obtuse angle. Two angles below 180° share each sine value, so check whether the answer should be obtuse, or use the cosine law instead.
- Dropping the minus sign in the cosine law. When $C$ is above 90°, $\cos C$ is negative and the third side gets longer.
