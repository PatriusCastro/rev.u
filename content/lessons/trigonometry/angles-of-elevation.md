## Right-triangle ratios
For an acute angle $\theta$ in a right triangle, each ratio compares two sides:

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad \tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$

Pick the ratio that uses the side you know and the side you want. If you know the horizontal distance and want a height, that is $\tan\theta$.

## Elevation and depression
The **angle of elevation** is measured up from the horizontal to an object above you. The **angle of depression** is measured down from the horizontal to an object below you.

The angle of depression from a high point to a low point equals the angle of elevation from the low point back up. The two horizontals are parallel, so these are alternate angles.

## Worked example
A kite string 80 m long makes an angle of 50° with level ground. How high is the kite?

- The string is the hypotenuse, 80 m. The height is opposite the 50° angle.
- $\sin 50° = h / 80$, so $h = 80 \sin 50°$.
- $h = 80(0.7660) \approx 61.28$ m.

## Two observation points
When a height is sighted from two points on one line, write the height from each point and solve them together. Call the unknown distance from the nearer point $x$ and the gap between the points $d$:

$$h = x\tan\beta \qquad h = (x + d)\tan\alpha$$

## Common slips
- The calculator is in radian mode. Check that $\sin 30° = 0.5$ first.
- The line of sight is the hypotenuse, not the ground distance.
- Rounding too early. Keep full values until the last step.
