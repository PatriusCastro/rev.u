topic: hydraulics/fluid-flow
title: Flow split between parallel pipes
answer: A
hint: Both branches lose the same head. Write each loss as $kQ^2$ and compare the two $k$ values.

A total flow of 200 L/s divides between two pipes laid in parallel between the same two junctions. Pipe A is 500 m long, 300 mm in diameter, with $f = 0.02$. Pipe B is 800 m long, 250 mm in diameter, with $f = 0.022$. Neglecting minor losses, how much flow goes through pipe A? Use $g = 9.81$ m/s².

A. 135.3 L/s    B. 64.7 L/s    C. 162.8 L/s    D. 100.0 L/s

## Solution
Write $h_f = kQ^2$ with $k = \dfrac{0.0826\,fL}{D^5}$: $k_A = \dfrac{0.0826(0.02)(500)}{0.3^5} = 340.0$ and $k_B = \dfrac{0.0826(0.022)(800)}{0.25^5} = 1489$.
---
Equal head loss: $k_AQ_A^2 = k_BQ_B^2$, so $Q_A = Q_B\sqrt{\dfrac{1489}{340.0}} = 2.093\,Q_B$.
---
$Q_A + Q_B = 0.2$, so $Q_B = \dfrac{0.2}{3.093} = 0.0647$ m³/s.
---
$Q_A = 0.2 - 0.0647 = 0.1353$ m³/s $= 135.3$ L/s. Check: both losses come out at 6.23 m.
